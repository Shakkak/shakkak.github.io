The problem

A model that predicts how a brain responds to an image is usually trained on many images from one person. A new person has only a few hundred labelled images to offer. This project asks how far those few hundred can go when the model is allowed to borrow from other people.

The approach

  1. Borrow. Average encoders trained on other people to get a first prediction of the new person’s response.
  2. Personalize. Learn a small linear map from the new person’s labelled images. It corrects the borrowed average across EEG sensors or fMRI vertices.

The main comparisons were written into version-controlled protocols before scoring, and each was scored once. The data are lab EEG (THINGS-EEG1 and THINGS-EEG2), consumer EEG (Alljoined-1.6M), 7-tesla fMRI (NSD) and single-trial 3-tesla fMRI (NOD).

Scope of the tests

Not every test used people who were new to the analysis. THINGS-EEG1’s first test and its three follow-ups were scored on the same 24 people. THINGS-EEG2’s ten people had been used to build the baselines and set the margin, and the Alljoined people were selected on test-split reliability. No model had been scored on the NSD and NOD people before their tests.

Where to look

  • The protocols are in docs/proposals/, and PROVENANCE.md lists the commit that holds each protocol and the commit that scored it.
  • Result tables are in results/, and docs/REPRODUCE.md maps every figure and table to the script or notebook that makes it.
  • Every analysis is a notebook in notebooks/.
